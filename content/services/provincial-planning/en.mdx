<Section alt icon="bank" title="About the Office" intro="What the Provincial Planning and Development Coordinator does under the law">

<Prose>

The Provincial Planning and Development Office (PPDO) prepares the plans that guide the Province's growth. Under the Local Government Code of 1991 (Republic Act No. 7160), the provincial planning and development coordinator formulates integrated economic, social, physical and other development plans and policies, prepares the Province's investment programs, and monitors and evaluates how plans are carried out.

The PPDO serves as the staff of the Provincial Development Council and works closely with the Budget Office and the Sangguniang Panlalawigan. It also collects and keeps socio-economic and physical data for the Province, which makes it a first stop for planning information.

</Prose>

<Section icon="list-check" title="What the Office Does" intro="General description of its services. See the Citizen's Charter for the official list.">

<Cards>

<Card icon="people" title="To the Public">

- Provides information on the Province's development plans and priorities.
- Supports public consultation by the Provincial Development Council, which includes civil society representatives.
- Can provide socio-economic and demographic data on the Province, subject to the office's data-sharing rules.

</Card>

<Card icon="diagram-3" title="To Other Provincial Offices">

- Leads the preparation of the Provincial Development and Physical Framework Plan and the Annual Investment Program.
- Reviews and helps prepare project proposals for funding.
- Monitors and evaluates programs and projects against the plan.
- Maintains the Province's planning data and maps.

</Card>

<Card icon="buildings" title="To Cities and Municipalities">

- Gives technical assistance on local development planning and land use planning when asked.
- Helps align municipal and city plans with the provincial plan.
- Each city and municipality has its own planning and development coordinator and prepares its own plans.

</Card>

</Cards>

<Section alt icon="list-ol" title="How to Request Planning Information" intro="General steps. See the Citizen's Charter for the official steps.">

<Columns>

<Box title="Steps">

1. Decide what you need: a plan, a map, a statistic, or a view on a proposed project.
2. Contact the PPDO by phone or visit the office at the Capitol, and say what you need and how you will use it.
3. The office will tell you whether the information is available, whether it can be shared, and what form of request it needs.
4. For official data sets and plans, you may be asked for a written request.

</Box>

<Box title="What to Bring">

- A valid government-issued ID
- A short written request that says what you need and why
- If you represent an organization or school: a letter or endorsement from it

</Box>

</Columns>
